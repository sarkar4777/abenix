THIS LONG-TERM LNG SALE AND PURCHASE AGREEMENT (hereinafter referred to as
the "Agreement") is entered into on the 15th day of November, 2023 (the
"Execution Date"),

BETWEEN:

(1) QATARGAS OPERATING COMPANY LIMITED, a limited liability company
    established under the laws of the State of Qatar, having its registered
    office at Qatargas Tower, Al Corniche Street, West Bay, Doha, State of
    Qatar, P.O. Box 22666 (hereinafter referred to as the "Seller"); and

(2) ENGIE SA, a societe anonyme organized and existing under the laws of
    the French Republic, having its registered office at 1 Place Samuel de
    Champlain, 92400 Courbevoie, France, registered with the Trade and
    Companies Register of Nanterre under number 542 107 651 (hereinafter
    referred to as the "Buyer").

The Seller and the Buyer are hereinafter collectively referred to as the
"Parties" and individually as a "Party".

RECITALS

WHEREAS, the Seller is the operator of the Qatargas liquefied natural gas
production facilities in Ras Laffan Industrial City, State of Qatar, with
aggregate liquefaction capacity in excess of 77 million tonnes per annum
(mtpa), and is one of the world's largest producers and exporters of LNG;

WHEREAS, QatarEnergy (formerly Qatar Petroleum), the state hydrocarbon
company of the State of Qatar, holds an indirect majority interest in the
Seller and its upstream gas production assets, including the North Field,
which contains the world's largest non-associated natural gas reserves
(estimated at approximately 900 trillion cubic feet);

WHEREAS, the Seller is currently expanding its LNG production capacity
through the North Field East (NFE) and North Field South (NFS) expansion
projects, which will increase Qatar's total LNG production capacity to
approximately 126 mtpa by 2028;

WHEREAS, the Buyer is a leading European integrated energy company
headquartered in France, engaged in the production, procurement, trading,
transportation, distribution, and sale of natural gas and LNG across Europe
and globally;

WHEREAS, the Buyer owns and operates LNG receiving and regasification
terminal infrastructure in France, including interests in the Fos Cavaou
LNG terminal and the Montoir-de-Bretagne LNG terminal;

WHEREAS, the European Union has identified the diversification of natural
gas supply sources and the development of LNG import infrastructure as
critical objectives for ensuring energy security and reducing dependence
on any single supplier, consistent with the EU Energy Security Strategy
and the REPowerEU Plan;

WHEREAS, the Parties wish to enter into this long-term Agreement for the
sale and purchase of LNG on the terms and conditions set forth herein,
reflecting the mutual commercial interests of the Parties and the long-term
strategic relationship between the State of Qatar and the French Republic
in the energy sector;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants, representations,
warranties, and agreements contained herein, and for other good and valuable
consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged,
the Parties agree as follows:

                    ARTICLE 1: DEFINITIONS AND INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

1.1 DEFINITIONS

In this Agreement, unless the context otherwise requires, the following
capitalized terms shall have the meanings ascribed to them below:

"ACQ" or "Annual Contract Quantity" means three million (3,000,000) tonnes
of LNG per Contract Year, subject to adjustment in accordance with this
Agreement.

"Actual Delivery Quantity" means the quantity of LNG (in tonnes) actually
delivered by the Seller and received by the Buyer at the Delivery Point
during any period.

"Affiliate" means, with respect to any Person, any other Person that
directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common
control with, such Person.

"ADP" or "Annual Delivery Programme" means the schedule of LNG cargo
deliveries for a Contract Year, established in accordance with Article 5.

"Applicable Law" means all laws, statutes, regulations, rules, orders,
decrees, directives, and requirements of any Governmental Authority
applicable to the Parties, the LNG, or the performance of obligations
under this Agreement, including the laws of the State of Qatar, the laws
of the French Republic, and applicable EU law.

"Berth" means the LNG unloading berth at the Delivery Terminal designated
for the receipt of LNG Carriers delivering LNG under this Agreement.

"Boil-Off Gas" or "BOG" means the natural gas generated by the evaporation
of LNG during storage, transportation, and unloading operations.

"British Thermal Unit" or "BTU" means the quantity of heat required to
raise the temperature of one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit at
or near 39.2 degrees Fahrenheit, at standard atmospheric pressure.

"Business Day" means any day other than a Saturday, Sunday, or a public
holiday in both Doha, Qatar and Paris, France.

"Cargo" means a full shipload quantity of LNG loaded onto and transported
by an LNG Carrier.

"CIF" means Cost, Insurance, and Freight (Incoterms 2020).

"Contract Price" shall have the meaning set forth in Article 4.1.

"Contract Year" means each period of twelve (12) consecutive calendar months
commencing on January 1 and ending on December 31 during the Term. The first
Contract Year shall commence on the Effective Date.

"DES" means Delivered Ex-Ship (equivalent to DAP -- Delivered at Place --

between the LNG Carrier's unloading manifold and the receiving terminal's

"Delivery Period" means the period, not exceeding seventy-two (72) hours

"Delivery Point" means the point at which title to and risk in the LNG

"Delivery Terminal" means the Fos Cavaou LNG receiving and regasification

"Diversion" shall have the meaning set forth in Article 6.

"Diversion Cargo" means a Cargo that the Buyer elects to divert to an

"DQT" or "Downward Quantity Tolerance" means the quantity of LNG by which

"Effective Date" means January 1, 2024.

"Force Majeure" shall have the meaning set forth in Article 10.

"Governmental Authority" means any government, governmental department,

"Gross Calorific Value" or "GCV" means the quantity of heat (in BTU or MJ)

conditions, with the water formed by combustion being condensed to liquid.

"Henry Hub" or "HH" means the Henry Hub natural gas pricing point located
in Erath, Louisiana, United States, as published by the Intercontinental

"LNG" means liquefied natural gas, being natural gas that has been cooled

"LNG Carrier" means an ocean-going vessel designed and constructed for the

"Make-Up" shall have the meaning set forth in Article 3.5.

"mtpa" means million tonnes per annum.

"Take-or-Pay" shall have the meaning set forth in Article 3.3.

"Term" shall have the meaning set forth in Article 2.1.

"Wobbe Index" means the Gross Calorific Value of the gas divided by the

                    ARTICLE 2: TERM
================================================================================

2.1 DURATION

This Agreement shall become effective on the Effective Date (January 1,
2024) and shall remain in full force and effect for a period of twenty (20)
Contract Years, expiring on December 31, 2043, unless earlier terminated in
accordance with this Agreement (the "Term").

2.2 EXTENSION

The Parties may extend the Term by mutual written agreement. Either Party
wishing to extend the Term shall notify the other Party in writing not less
than thirty-six (36) months prior to the expiration of the Term. The Parties
shall negotiate in good faith the terms and conditions of any extension,
including pricing.

2.3 RAMP-UP PERIOD

(a) Contract Year 1 (2024): The ACQ shall be reduced to two million
(2,000,000) tonnes to allow for the phasing of supply arrangements and
shipping logistics.

(b) Contract Year 2 (2025): The ACQ shall be increased to two million five
hundred thousand (2,500,000) tonnes.

(c) Contract Year 3 (2026) and thereafter: The full ACQ of three million
(3,000,000) tonnes shall apply.


================================================================================
               ARTICLE 3: QUANTITY AND TAKE-OR-PAY
================================================================================

3.1 ANNUAL CONTRACT QUANTITY

(a) Subject to the ramp-up provisions in Article 2.3, the Seller shall make
available for delivery, and the Buyer shall take or pay for, three million
(3,000,000) tonnes of LNG per Contract Year (the "ACQ").

(b) The ACQ corresponds to approximately fifty-four (54) Cargoes per
Contract Year, each Cargo being approximately 55,500 tonnes (loaded on a
Q-Max Carrier with a capacity of 266,000 m3 at a nominal LNG density of
approximately 450 kg/m3, accounting for heel retention and cargo measurement
tolerances).

3.2 QUANTITY TOLERANCES

(a) Upward Quantity Tolerance ("UQT"): The Seller shall have the right (but
not the obligation) to deliver up to one hundred and ten percent (110%) of
the ACQ in any Contract Year. Additional Cargoes above the ACQ shall be
subject to the Buyer's acceptance and berth availability at the Delivery
Terminal.

(b) Downward Quantity Tolerance ("DQT"): The minimum quantity that the
Buyer must take (or pay for, in the event of failure to take) in each
Contract Year shall be eighty-five percent (85%) of the ACQ, equal to
two million five hundred and fifty thousand (2,550,000) tonnes (the
"Minimum Take-or-Pay Quantity").

3.3 TAKE-OR-PAY OBLIGATION

(a) If the Actual Delivery Quantity received by the Buyer in any Contract
Year is less than the Minimum Take-or-Pay Quantity (85% of ACQ), the Buyer
shall pay to the Seller, within sixty (60) days after the end of such
Contract Year, a take-or-pay payment ("Take-or-Pay Payment") equal to:

    Take-or-Pay Payment = (Minimum Take-or-Pay Quantity - Actual Delivery
    Quantity) x Average Contract Price for such Contract Year

Where "Average Contract Price" means the weighted average price per tonne
of LNG delivered during the Contract Year, calculated using the pricing
formula in Article 4.1.

(b) The Take-or-Pay obligation shall not apply to shortfalls in delivery
caused by:
    (i) Force Majeure affecting the Buyer;
    (ii) the Seller's failure to make LNG available for delivery;
    (iii) Off-Specification LNG rejected by the Buyer in accordance with
          Article 7;
    (iv) Seller-caused delays at the Loading Port;
    (v) Diversion Cargoes diverted with the Seller's consent (to the extent
        such diversions are deducted from the ACQ).

3.4 SHIP-OR-PAY OBLIGATION

(a) If the Seller fails to make available for delivery to the Buyer in any
Contract Year a quantity of LNG equal to or greater than eighty-five percent
(85%) of the ACQ (the "Minimum Ship-or-Pay Quantity"), the Seller shall pay
to the Buyer, within sixty (60) days after the end of such Contract Year, a
ship-or-pay payment ("Ship-or-Pay Payment") equal to:

    Ship-or-Pay Payment = (Minimum Ship-or-Pay Quantity - Quantity Made
    Available) x Average Contract Price for such Contract Year

(b) The Ship-or-Pay obligation shall not apply to shortfalls caused by:
    (i) Force Majeure affecting the Seller;
    (ii) the Buyer's failure to accept or take delivery of LNG;
    (iii) closure or unavailability of the Delivery Terminal not caused by
          the Seller;
    (iv) Buyer-directed diversions.

3.5 MAKE-UP RIGHTS

(a) In any Contract Year in which the Buyer has made a Take-or-Pay Payment,
the Buyer shall have the right, in any subsequent Contract Year during the
Term (the "Make-Up Year"), to take delivery of additional LNG (up to the
quantity for which Take-or-Pay was paid) at no additional cost (i.e.,
without paying the Contract Price for such additional LNG), subject to:
    (i) the Buyer's Make-Up right being exercised on a first-in, first-out
        basis;
    (ii) the Make-Up quantity not exceeding ten percent (10%) of the ACQ in
         any Make-Up Year (i.e., 300,000 tonnes);
    (iii) the Seller's ability to supply the Make-Up quantity from available
          production capacity;
    (iv) berth availability at the Delivery Terminal; and
    (v) the Make-Up right being exercised within five (5) Contract Years of
        the Contract Year in which the Take-or-Pay Payment was made,
        failing which the Make-Up right shall lapse.

(b) The Buyer shall give the Seller not less than ninety (90) days' advance
notice of its intention to exercise Make-Up rights in respect of any
Contract Year.


================================================================================
               ARTICLE 4: PRICING
================================================================================

4.1 CONTRACT PRICE

The price per MMBtu of LNG delivered at the Delivery Point (the "Contract
Price") shall be calculated for each Cargo as follows:

    Contract Price (USD/MMBtu) = HH + S + F

Where:
    HH = the Henry Hub Natural Gas Price, being the arithmetic average of
         the NYMEX Henry Hub Natural Gas Futures Contract settlement prices
         for the front-month contract during the Pricing Period;

    S  = the Fixed Adder, equal to three dollars and fifty cents per MMBtu
         (USD 3.50/MMBtu), representing the liquefaction tolling fee and
         margin component;

    F  = the Shipping Component, being the Platts-assessed freight cost for
         LNG transportation from Ras Laffan, Qatar to Fos-sur-Mer, France,
         on a DES basis, for the relevant voyage, as published by S&P Global
         Commodity Insights (Platts) for the "AG-Mediterranean" route or the
         nearest comparable route assessment, expressed in USD/MMBtu.

4.2 PRICING PERIOD

The "Pricing Period" for each Cargo shall be the calendar month in which the
Bill of Lading Date falls (i.e., the month in which the Cargo is loaded at
the Loading Port).

4.3 PRICE CONVERSION

For the purpose of invoicing, the Contract Price (in USD/MMBtu) shall be
converted to a price per tonne of LNG using the actual measured Gross
Calorific Value of the delivered Cargo:

    Invoice Amount (USD) = Contract Price (USD/MMBtu) x Total GCV of Cargo
    (MMBtu)

Where Total GCV of Cargo = Volume of LNG delivered (in Nm3) x GCV per Nm3
(BTU/Nm3) / 1,000,000

4.4 ANNUAL PRICE REVIEW

(a) Commencing from Contract Year 5 (2028), either Party may request a
review of the Fixed Adder (S) component of the Contract Price by giving
written notice to the other Party at least twelve (12) months prior to the
start of the relevant Contract Year.

(b) The purpose of the price review shall be to ensure that the Fixed Adder
remains reflective of prevailing market conditions for long-term LNG supply
contracts from the Gulf region to Europe. The Parties shall negotiate in
good faith, taking into account:
    (i) the prevailing level of LNG spot and term contract prices;
    (ii) the prices in comparable long-term LNG contracts concluded in the
         preceding three (3) years;
    (iii) changes in the cost of LNG liquefaction and transportation; and
    (iv) the overall commercial balance of this Agreement.

(c) If the Parties are unable to agree on a revised Fixed Adder within six
(6) months of the commencement of negotiations, the matter shall be referred
to an independent expert for determination in accordance with Article 13.2.
The expert's determination of the Fixed Adder shall be final and binding.

(d) Pending the conclusion of the price review, the existing Contract Price
shall continue to apply, and any adjustment shall be retroactively applied
from the date the price review was requested, with interest on any
retroactive amounts at SOFR.

4.5 INVOICING AND PAYMENT

(a) The Seller shall issue an invoice to the Buyer for each Cargo within
ten (10) Business Days after the completion of unloading at the Delivery
Terminal.

(b) Each invoice shall specify:
    (i) the Cargo identification number;
    (ii) the name of the LNG Carrier;
    (iii) the Bill of Lading Date and the date of delivery;
    (iv) the volume and mass of LNG delivered (as measured);
    (v) the Gross Calorific Value of the LNG delivered;
    (vi) the total energy content (in MMBtu);
    (vii) the Henry Hub price applicable during the Pricing Period;
    (viii) the Fixed Adder;
    (ix) the Shipping Component;
    (x) the Contract Price (USD/MMBtu);
    (xi) the total invoice amount (USD); and
    (xii) bank account details for payment.

(c) Payment shall be due within thirty (30) days of the date of the invoice
by wire transfer of immediately available funds in United States Dollars to
the Seller's designated bank account.

(d) Late payment shall bear interest at SOFR plus two percent (2%) per
annum, calculated on a daily compounding basis from the due date until the
date of actual payment.


================================================================================
               ARTICLE 5: SCHEDULING AND NOMINATIONS
================================================================================

5.1 ANNUAL DELIVERY PROGRAMME

(a) Not less than ninety (90) days prior to the start of each Contract Year,
the Seller shall propose, and the Parties shall agree upon, an Annual
Delivery Programme (ADP) setting forth the number of Cargoes, the
approximate delivery dates (within a window of plus or minus five (5)
days), the LNG Carrier to be used for each delivery, and the estimated
volume and quality of each Cargo.

(b) The ADP shall be consistent with the ACQ (or the adjusted ACQ for the
ramp-up period) and shall take into account:
    (i) the Seller's production and loading schedules;
    (ii) berth availability at the Delivery Terminal;
    (iii) the Buyer's regasification and send-out requirements;
    (iv) scheduled maintenance at the Seller's Facilities and the Delivery
         Terminal; and
    (v) LNG Carrier availability and voyage times.

5.2 NINETY-DAY ROLLING SCHEDULE

At least ninety (90) days before the expected delivery date of each Cargo,
the Seller shall provide the Buyer with a preliminary Cargo nomination
("90-Day Nomination"), specifying:
    (i) the expected loading date window (5-day window);
    (ii) the expected delivery date window (5-day window);
    (iii) the identity of the LNG Carrier;
    (iv) the estimated volume and quality of the Cargo.

5.3 THIRTY-DAY CONFIRMATION

At least thirty (30) days before the expected delivery date, the Seller
shall confirm the Cargo nomination ("30-Day Confirmation"), narrowing the
delivery window to plus or minus three (3) days.

5.4 SEVEN-DAY FINAL NOMINATION

At least seven (7) days before the expected delivery date, the Seller shall
provide a final nomination ("7-Day Final Nomination"), specifying:
    (i) the confirmed delivery date (plus or minus one (1) day);
    (ii) the confirmed LNG Carrier;
    (iii) the estimated time of arrival (ETA);
    (iv) the estimated volume and quality of the Cargo; and
    (v) any special requirements for unloading.

5.5 SCHEDULE CHANGES

(a) Either Party may request changes to the delivery schedule, subject to
reasonable notice and the other Party's acceptance (which shall not be
unreasonably withheld).

(b) The Seller shall give the Buyer at least fourteen (14) days' notice of
any material change to a Cargo nomination (including a change of LNG
Carrier, a delay of more than two (2) days, or a change of more than ten
percent (10%) in estimated Cargo volume).

(c) If the Delivery Terminal is unable to receive a Cargo on the nominated
delivery date due to berth unavailability, weather restrictions, or terminal
outage, the Buyer shall notify the Seller as soon as practicable and the
Parties shall cooperate to reschedule the delivery.


================================================================================
               ARTICLE 6: DIVERSION RIGHTS
================================================================================

6.1 BUYER'S DIVERSION RIGHT

(a) The Buyer shall have the right to divert up to twenty percent (20%) of
the ACQ per Contract Year (i.e., up to 600,000 tonnes, approximately
10-11 Cargoes) to alternative LNG receiving terminals other than the
Delivery Terminal ("Alternative Delivery Points"), subject to the following
conditions:

    (i) the Buyer shall give the Seller at least thirty (30) days' written
        notice of its intention to divert a Cargo, specifying the
        Alternative Delivery Point, the estimated arrival date, and the
        identity of the receiving terminal;

    (ii) the Alternative Delivery Point must be a recognized LNG receiving
         terminal capable of safely receiving the nominated LNG Carrier, with
         adequate draft, berth dimensions, and compatible unloading equipment;

    (iii) the LNG quality specifications at the Alternative Delivery Point
          must be compatible with the quality of the LNG being delivered
          (or the Buyer shall bear the risk and cost of any quality issues);

    (iv) the diversion shall not result in any incremental cost or liability
         to the Seller (any additional shipping costs, port charges, or
         delays shall be borne by the Buyer);

    (v) the diversion shall not violate any Applicable Law, trade sanction,
        or embargo applicable to the Seller, the LNG Carrier, or the LNG;
        and

    (vi) the Buyer shall not divert any Cargo to a destination or end-user
         subject to trade sanctions imposed by the State of Qatar, the
         United Nations, or any jurisdiction to which the Seller is subject.

(b) Title to and risk in a Diversion Cargo shall pass from the Seller to
the Buyer at the Delivery Point of the Alternative Delivery Point (the
flange connection between the LNG Carrier's manifold and the alternative
terminal's unloading arm).

6.2 DIVERSION PROFIT SHARING

(a) If the Buyer diverts a Cargo and resells the LNG at a price exceeding
the Contract Price applicable to such Cargo, the Buyer shall share any
net profit (after deduction of incremental transportation, port, and
transaction costs) with the Seller as follows:

    Seller's Share: Fifty percent (50%) of the net diversion profit
    Buyer's Share: Fifty percent (50%) of the net diversion profit

(b) The Buyer shall provide the Seller with a statement of diversion
economics within sixty (60) days after completion of the diverted delivery,
and any diversion profit-sharing payment shall be due within thirty (30)
days thereafter.

6.3 DESTINATION RESTRICTIONS

(a) Notwithstanding the Buyer's diversion rights, the Buyer shall not,
without the prior written consent of the Seller, divert or resell any LNG
to any country or territory that is subject to comprehensive trade
sanctions or embargoes imposed by the State of Qatar or any major
international body.

(b) The Buyer shall provide the Seller with destination confirmation for
each Diversion Cargo, including the identity of the receiving terminal,
the name of the end-buyer (if applicable), and an end-use certificate
if requested by the Seller.


================================================================================
               ARTICLE 7: LNG QUALITY SPECIFICATIONS
================================================================================

7.1 QUALITY SPECIFICATIONS

The LNG delivered at the Delivery Point shall conform to the following
quality specifications (the "Quality Specifications"):

Parameter                        Minimum         Maximum         Unit
-----------                      ----------      ----------      ----
Gross Calorific Value (GCV)      1,050           1,150           BTU/scf
Wobbe Index                      1,300           1,400           BTU/scf
Methane (CH4)                    85.0            --              mol %
Ethane (C2H6)                    --              8.0             mol %
Propane (C3H8)                   --              3.5             mol %
Butanes and heavier (C4+)        --              2.0             mol %
Nitrogen (N2)                    --              1.0             mol %
Carbon Dioxide (CO2)             --              50              ppmv
Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S)           --              3.3             mg/Nm3
Total Sulfur                     --              30              mg/Nm3
Mercury (Hg)                     --              0.01            ug/Nm3
Carbonyl Sulfide (COS)           --              5               ppmv
Water Content                    --              0.1             ppmv
Temperature at delivery          --              -155            degrees C
Free Water and Solids            None            None            --

7.2 MEASUREMENT AND ANALYSIS

(a) The quality of each Cargo shall be determined by analysis of
representative LNG samples taken during unloading at the Delivery Terminal
using continuous composite sampling methods in accordance with ISO 8943
(Refrigerated Light Hydrocarbon Fluids -- Sampling of Liquefied Natural
Gas -- Continuous and Intermittent Methods) or equivalent standard.

(b) The LNG samples shall be analyzed using gas chromatography in accordance
with ISO 6974 (Natural Gas -- Determination of Composition and Associated
Uncertainty by Gas Chromatography) or GPA 2261 standard.

(c) The Gross Calorific Value shall be calculated from the compositional
analysis in accordance with ISO 6976 (Natural Gas -- Calculation of
Calorific Values, Density, Relative Density, and Wobbe Indices from
Composition).

7.3 OFF-SPECIFICATION LNG

(a) If the Seller tenders LNG that does not conform to the Quality
Specifications, the Buyer shall have the following rights:

    (i) Accept the Off-Specification LNG at a mutually agreed price
        adjustment reflecting the quality deviation, provided that the
        Off-Specification LNG can be safely received, stored, and processed
        at the Delivery Terminal without adverse effect on terminal equipment
        or downstream gas quality;

    (ii) Reject the Off-Specification LNG, in which case the Seller shall
         bear all costs and liabilities associated with the rejected Cargo,
         including demurrage, shipping costs, and the cost of procuring
         replacement LNG;

    (iii) Accept the Off-Specification LNG and blend it with other LNG or
          gas at the Delivery Terminal to bring the resultant gas within the
          quality specifications of the French gas grid (operated by GRTgaz),
          with the cost of blending borne by the Seller.

(b) If the Buyer rejects an Off-Specification Cargo, such rejection shall
not count against the Buyer's Take-or-Pay obligation and the Seller shall
remain obligated to deliver a replacement Cargo within a reasonable time.


================================================================================
               ARTICLE 8: DELIVERY, TITLE, AND RISK
================================================================================

8.1 DELIVERY BASIS

All deliveries under this Agreement shall be on a DES (Delivered Ex-Ship)
basis at the Delivery Point (Fos Cavaou LNG Terminal). The Seller shall be
responsible for all costs and risks of transportation of LNG from the
Loading Port to the Delivery Point, including:
    (i) LNG Carrier chartering and operating costs;
    (ii) fuel and Boil-Off Gas consumption during the voyage;
    (iii) canal transit fees (including Suez Canal tolls);
    (iv) port charges at the Loading Port;
    (v) marine insurance for the cargo during transit; and
    (vi) all other costs associated with the voyage.

8.2 TITLE AND RISK

(a) Title to and risk in the LNG shall pass from the Seller to the Buyer
at the Delivery Point (the flange connection between the LNG Carrier's
unloading manifold and the Delivery Terminal's unloading arm) as the LNG
passes through such flange connection during unloading.

(b) Upon passage of title, the Buyer shall be responsible for the LNG,
including all costs of receiving, storing, regasifying, and distributing
the natural gas.

8.3 PORT CHARGES AT DELIVERY TERMINAL

(a) Port charges, pilotage, towage, and berthing fees at the Delivery
Terminal (Fos Cavaou) shall be borne by the Buyer.

(b) Demurrage at the Delivery Terminal shall be the responsibility of the
Buyer to the extent caused by delays attributable to the Buyer, the
Delivery Terminal, or port authorities. Demurrage shall be calculated at
the prevailing market rate for Q-Max LNG Carriers or, if no market rate
is available, at a rate of USD 150,000 per day.

               ARTICLE 10: FORCE MAJEURE
================================================================================

10.1 DEFINITION

"Force Majeure" means any event or circumstance, or combination thereof,
that: (a) is beyond the reasonable control of the Affected Party; (b) could
not have been prevented or avoided by the Affected Party through the
exercise of reasonable diligence; and (c) directly and materially prevents
or delays the Affected Party from performing its obligations under this
Agreement.

10.2 FORCE MAJEURE EVENTS

Without limiting the generality of Section 10.1, Force Majeure Events
include:

(a) Natural Events:
    (i) earthquake, tsunami, volcanic eruption, hurricane, cyclone, or
        typhoon;
    (ii) flood, landslide, or subsidence;
    (iii) epidemic or pandemic;
    (iv) extreme weather conditions preventing safe navigation or port
         operations.

(b) Political and Governmental Events:
    (i) war (declared or undeclared), invasion, armed conflict, blockade,
        or military action;
    (ii) revolution, insurrection, civil unrest, terrorism, sabotage, or
         piracy;
    (iii) expropriation, nationalization, or confiscation by any
          Governmental Authority;
    (iv) trade embargo, economic sanctions, or import/export restrictions
         imposed by the United Nations, European Union, State of Qatar, or
         the French Republic;
    (v) act of any Governmental Authority, including denial, revocation, or
        non-renewal of any permit, license, or authorization required for
        performance.

(c) Infrastructure Events:
    (i) Suez Canal blockage, closure, or restriction (including but not
        limited to grounding incidents, security closures, or maintenance
        shutdowns exceeding forty-eight (48) hours);
    (ii) closure of the Port of Fos-sur-Mer or the Fos Cavaou LNG Terminal
         due to weather, labor action, equipment failure, or regulatory
         order;
    (iii) major failure of the Seller's liquefaction facilities at Ras
          Laffan not caused by inadequate maintenance;
    (iv) closure of the Port of Ras Laffan;
    (v) failure of the French gas transmission network (operated by GRTgaz
        or Terega) preventing gas offtake from the Delivery Terminal.

(d) Labor Events:
    (i) general or industry-wide strike, lockout, or labor action (not
        limited to the workforce of either Party or its contractors).

10.3 EXCLUSIONS

The following shall not constitute Force Majeure:
(a) changes in market prices, currency fluctuations, or loss of profit;
(b) inability to obtain financing;
(c) failure of a subcontractor or supplier (unless caused by Force Majeure);
(d) equipment breakdown due to inadequate maintenance or design defect;
(e) normal weather conditions (including seasonal fog at Fos-sur-Mer);
(f) changes in law or regulation (which are addressed in Article 12).

10.4 NOTIFICATION

The Affected Party shall notify the other Party within seventy-two (72)
hours (three (3) calendar days) of becoming aware of the Force Majeure
Event, specifying the nature, expected duration, estimated impact on
performance, and the measures being taken to mitigate the effects.

10.5 CONSEQUENCES

(a) The Affected Party shall be relieved from liability for failure or
delay in performing its obligations to the extent caused by the Force
Majeure Event.

(b) Take-or-Pay and Ship-or-Pay obligations shall be reduced pro rata for
the duration of the Force Majeure Event.

(c) The Affected Party shall use all reasonable efforts to mitigate the
effects of the Force Majeure Event and to resume performance as soon as
practicable.

(d) If a Force Majeure Event continues for more than eighteen (18)
consecutive months, either Party may terminate this Agreement by giving
one hundred and eighty (180) days' written notice.

               SCHEDULE 5: INDICATIVE PRICING EXAMPLES
================================================================================

The following examples illustrate the Contract Price calculation under
various Henry Hub price scenarios:

Scenario 1: Low HH Price
    HH = USD 2.50/MMBtu
    S (Fixed Adder) = USD 3.50/MMBtu
    F (Shipping) = USD 1.80/MMBtu
    Contract Price = 2.50 + 3.50 + 1.80 = USD 7.80/MMBtu
    Approx. price per tonne = 7.80 x 48.7 MMBtu/tonne = USD 379.86/tonne

Scenario 2: Medium HH Price
    HH = USD 3.50/MMBtu
    S (Fixed Adder) = USD 3.50/MMBtu
    F (Shipping) = USD 1.80/MMBtu
    Contract Price = 3.50 + 3.50 + 1.80 = USD 8.80/MMBtu
    Approx. price per tonne = 8.80 x 48.7 MMBtu/tonne = USD 428.56/tonne

Scenario 3: High HH Price
    HH = USD 5.00/MMBtu
    S (Fixed Adder) = USD 3.50/MMBtu
    F (Shipping) = USD 2.20/MMBtu
    Contract Price = 5.00 + 3.50 + 2.20 = USD 10.70/MMBtu
    Approx. price per tonne = 10.70 x 48.7 MMBtu/tonne = USD 521.09/tonne

Note: The conversion factor of approximately 48.7 MMBtu per tonne is based
on a typical Qatari LNG composition with a GCV of approximately 1,100
BTU/scf. The actual conversion factor will be determined for each Cargo
based on the measured compositional analysis.
